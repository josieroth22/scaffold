# Competitive Landscape, October 2026

Web research done October 14-15, 2026, while Scaffold was paused. Sources are linked inline. Many comparison articles in this space are written by competing vendors (Orbit publishes a lot of them), so treat prices and feature claims as marketing until checked. None of these products were tested hands-on.

## The short version

The field is crowded, but almost no one occupies Scaffold's exact position. Nearly every competitor is either a free or subscription **chat tool aimed at students**, or a **data tool with no strategy**. Searches turned up no one selling a one-time, verified-data plan written for parents.

Scaffold's corner: **parent-facing, one-time, verified data, deep financial aid and merit coverage.** That corner is mostly empty. The open question is whether it's empty because it's an opening or because there's little demand; launching is how to find out.

## Market signals

- **Demand is real and mainstream.** [EAB's 2026 parent survey](https://eab.com/about/newsroom/press/2026-parent-survey/) (2,515 parents): 57% have used AI like ChatGPT to evaluate colleges; 34% found schools they hadn't considered. Covered this summer by [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/ai-gains-ground-as-a-college-search-tool-for-parents/2026/08) and the [Christian Science Monitor](https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Education/2026/0803/artificial-intelligence-college-search). Scaffold's biggest competitor is still free ChatGPT.
- **The press is warning parents about the problem Scaffold solves.** [Parenting for College](https://www.parentingforcollege.com/2026/09/24/ai-built-college-list/) cautions that AI-built lists rely on stale aid figures. Parents are being primed to look for verified numbers.
- **The format appears unclaimed.** A search for one-time personalized college plans at $49, $99, or $199 found only subscriptions, consulting packages, and generic guides ([example](https://collegeaidpro.com/college-planning-services-pricing)). A separate search found no tool combining income, merit aid, and a ranked list in one place.

## Deep dive: PrepToDone

Reviewed from its homepage only (not the tool or its sample document).

- **Product:** answers one question, "Should you apply Early Decision to the school you're leaning toward?" Free tier: published ED vs regular admit rates and SAT position. Paid ($9.99 through Nov 15, "regular" $49): an ED recommendation, the odds that every school says no, a cost-at-your-income table, up to two suggested schools, three re-scores within 90 days. One-page document; no account; 14-day refund.
- **Trust design (their strongest feature):** "We don't publish numbers we can't source"; every figure links to its source; shows where a school publishes nothing; predictions written to an unchangeable record and graded publicly each December, misses included. Cites a source (IECA fee survey) for its consultant-price comparison.
- **Data:** ED filings (104 read), Dept. of Education, Scorecard, IPEDS; 570 colleges priced, 542 with net price by income, 483 deadlines verified for 2026-27. Deadlines are something Scaffold does not systematically verify.
- **Gaps:** no financial aid or scholarship guidance; seniors only, in a roughly six-week season; anonymous team ("ex-Google · Meta · Apple"); no testimonials. ED tends to suit full-pay families, the opposite of Scaffold's mission lead.
- **Overlap with Scaffold:** its whole product is one section of a Scaffold plan (the application-round recommendation). Not a substitute for families who care about cost.
- **Pricing signal:** its "regular" $49 lands on Scaffold's $50, suggesting ~$50 is the market's sense of a data-driven college document. Watch whether it ever actually sells at $49; it may be a reference price for the sale.

## Where Scaffold wins

1. **Parents.** Nearly every competitor talks to students; EAB shows parents doing the research. Scaffold addresses parents by name.
2. **Done for you.** Next to $40-80/month subscriptions, $50 once is cheap; next to free chat, it needs the pitch "a finished plan, not an app you have to drive."
3. **Financial aid and merit depth.** Competitors either skip it (PrepToDone), estimate it weakly (College Raptor), or shut down (Going Merry). The July 2026 retro-test showed Scaffold finding the exact scholarship a real student won.
4. **Long horizon.** Plans for 9th-11th graders, not just seniors in deadline season.
5. **Founder story.** "I tested it on my seventeen-year-old self, and it found my scholarship by name" beats an anonymous team.

## Where Scaffold is exposed

- **Free is the default price** for "AI counselor" (CollegeVine, BigFuture, ChatGPT). Scaffold has no free tier beyond sample plans.
- **Merit coverage has known holes.** The retro-test found real full rides missing from Scaffold's reference data (UNC beyond Morehead/Robertson, WashU beyond Danforth/Ervin/Rodriguez, Duke's AB Duke). The moat is only as deep as this data.
- **Easy to copy the format.** A subscription tool could add a "generate full report" button; a data tool like College Raptor could add an AI narrative.
- **Paused through peak season.** PrepToDone launched into ED season with deadline-tied pricing; Scaffold is missing it. Next demand wave: January to March (regular-decision results, aid offers).

## What to borrow (mapped to the roadmap)

1. **A free tool as the way in.** A free "what will [school] actually cost at my income?" lookup, built on data Scaffold already has (net price by income for 1,492 schools). Upgrades the static SEO pages planned for Launch Plan chunk 7: same work, but it also collects emails and earns trust before anyone pays.
2. **Show sources, don't just claim them.** Move up the existing roadmap item "Add source citations to output." Per-number sources answer the "is this just ChatGPT?" question better than any FAQ copy.
3. **Public, graded predictions.** Record Scaffold's admit-probability calls and grade them against reported outcomes. The Jocelyn retro-test is the one-off version. Needs families to report back, so it's a later-stage idea.
4. **Merit-coverage pass as a competitive priority.** Raise the data-hygiene work (QuestBridge flags, the three named merit gaps, Benacquisto status, school-record dedupe) from cleanup to moat-building.
5. **Cite the consultant comparison.** Scaffold's "$10,000 counselor" claim has no source; the IECA average ($5,838/package) is citable.
